## How to Invest in Data Center REITs: AI Infrastructure Meets Real Estate Income

Data centers used to be the unglamorous plumbing of the internet — warehouses full of servers that most investors ignored. That era is over. The explosion of AI workloads, cloud migration, and edge computing has turned data center real estate into one of the highest-growth property sectors in modern REIT history, and the numbers for 2026 confirm it.

U.S. data center construction starts exceeded $65 billion in the first half of 2026, according to CBRE, nearly doubling the full-year 2024 figure. Vacancy rates across major markets — Northern Virginia, Dallas, Phoenix, and Silicon Valley — sit below 3%, the tightest on record. And lease rates for AI-ready, high-density facilities have climbed 30-50% year-over-year as hyperscalers scramble for power and space.

For investors, this creates an unusually compelling setup: a real asset class with visible demand tailwinds, contractual revenue, and a structural supply constraint (you cannot build a data center without power, permits, and fiber interconnection, all of which take years to secure). Data center REITs let you own a slice of the physical infrastructure powering the AI revolution while collecting quarterly dividends.

This guide covers the investment thesis, the best data center REITs and ETFs available today, the risks you need to understand, and how to size a position in your portfolio.

---

## Key Takeaways

- **Data center REITs are the real estate play on AI infrastructure** — they own the physical buildings where cloud computing, AI training, and enterprise IT workloads run, generating revenue through long-term leases with creditworthy tenants.
- **Demand is structurally outpacing supply** — AI model training and inference require exponentially more compute capacity, and new data center construction is bottlenecked by power availability and permitting timelines.
- **The sector has delivered exceptional returns** — the FTSE Nareit Data Center REIT Index has outperformed the broader REIT index by over 20 percentage points annualized over the past three years.
- **Not all data center REITs are equal** — some focus on hyperscale wholesale leasing, others on colocation and interconnection, and the differences in business model drive meaningful valuation and risk profile differences.
- **A 5-10% portfolio allocation captures the growth while managing concentration risk** — data center REITs are volatile relative to other property sectors and trade at premium valuations.

## Why Data Center REITs Are Surging: The Three Demand Drivers

### 1. AI Training and Inference Are Consuming Unprecedented Compute Capacity

The compute requirements for frontier AI models are growing at roughly 4x per year. Training a state-of-the-art large language model in 2026 requires clusters of tens of thousands of GPUs running continuously for months, each consuming 500-1,000 watts — and that is just the training phase.

Inference — running these models in production for hundreds of millions of users — may ultimately consume even more total power than training. Morgan Stanley estimates that AI inference workloads alone could require 50-80 GW of new data center capacity globally by 2030, roughly equivalent to the entire current U.S. data center footprint.

This compute has to live somewhere physical. Every AI query you send to ChatGPT, every image Midjourney generates, every autonomous vehicle perception model that processes a frame of video — it all runs on servers sitting inside data centers. The companies that own those data centers are collecting rent on the AI revolution.

### 2. Cloud Migration Is Far From Over

Despite the attention AI receives, traditional cloud migration remains the single largest demand driver for data center space. Gartner estimates that only 35-40% of enterprise workloads have moved to public or hybrid cloud environments as of mid-2026. The remaining 60%+ represents years of ongoing migration demand.

Financial services, healthcare, government, and manufacturing — sectors with heavy regulatory requirements — are in the early-to-middle innings of cloud adoption. Each migration contract translates directly into leased data center capacity for the hyperscalers (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud), who in turn lease space from or build alongside REIT operators.

### 3. Edge Computing Is Creating New Markets

The rollout of AI-powered applications that require low latency — real-time translation, autonomous systems, AR/VR, smart manufacturing — is driving demand for smaller data center deployments closer to end users. This "edge" compute trend is opening new geographic markets and creating demand for smaller, distributed facilities that complement traditional hyperscale campuses.

Edge deployments tend to carry higher lease rates per kilowatt than hyperscale facilities because tenants are paying for proximity and low latency, not just raw space. For REITs with established edge networks, this represents a high-margin growth vector.

## The Best Data Center REITs to Invest In for 2026

### Equinix (EQIX) — The Global Interconnection Leader

**Market Cap:** ~$95 billion | **Dividend Yield:** ~1.8% | **2026 Forward FFO Multiple:** ~30x

Equinix is the largest data center REIT globally, operating over 260 facilities across 72 metros in 33 countries. But what sets Equinix apart is not size alone — it is the network effect of its interconnection ecosystem. Over 10,000 customers interconnect through Equinix facilities, creating switching costs that make tenant churn extremely low (historically below 2% quarterly).

**Why it matters for AI:** Equinix's xScale program, dedicated to hyperscale deployments, has delivered over 200 MW of capacity to AI-focused tenants since 2023. The company's global footprint also positions it to capture AI inference demand, which increasingly requires geographically distributed compute.

**Risk to watch:** Equinix trades at a premium valuation relative to peers. Any slowdown in AI capex or a significant increase in interest rates could compress the multiple.

### Digital Realty (DLR) — The Hyperscale Capacity Engine

**Market Cap:** ~$55 billion | **Dividend Yield:** ~2.8% | **2026 Forward FFO Multiple:** ~25x

Digital Realty is the second-largest data center REIT and the primary hyperscale wholesale provider. Its PlatformDIGITAL offering combines colocation, interconnection, and bare-metal services, but the growth engine is large-scale builds for cloud and AI customers.

**Why it matters for AI:** Digital Realty has secured over $15 billion in hyperscale pre-leasing commitments through 2028, driven heavily by AI training infrastructure demand. Its joint ventures in key markets (Northern Virginia, Dallas, Frankfurt) give it access to power allocations that would take competitors years to replicate.

**Risk to watch:** Digital Realty carries more balance sheet leverage than Equinix. In a rising-rate environment, its higher debt load creates more earnings sensitivity to interest expense.

### CyrusOne (CONE) — The AI-First Builder

**Market Cap:** ~$18 billion | **Dividend Yield:** ~2.2% | **2026 Forward FFO Multiple:** ~22x

After going private in 2022 and returning to public markets in 2025, CyrusOne has repositioned itself as the purpose-built AI data center developer. Its newer facilities are designed from the ground up for high-density AI workloads, with liquid cooling infrastructure, 50+ MW campus power, and direct fiber connectivity to major AI cloud on-ramps.

**Why it matters for AI:** CyrusOne's facilities are architected for the 40-80 kW per rack densities that modern GPU clusters demand, compared to the 8-15 kW per rack that traditional facilities support. This positions it to capture the highest-value AI training leases.

**Risk to watch:** As a smaller, more concentrated operator, CyrusOne has less geographic diversification and higher customer concentration than Equinix or Digital Realty.

### Iron Mountain (IRM) — The Diversified Data Play

**Market Cap:** ~$35 billion | **Dividend Yield:** ~2.4% | **2026 Forward FFO Multiple:** ~24x

Iron Mountain is not a pure-play data center REIT — it also operates records storage, asset lifecycle management, and information governance businesses. But its data center segment has been the growth engine, with revenue growing 25%+ annually as the company leverages its existing real estate footprint and customer relationships to build hyperscale and colocation facilities.

**Why it matters for AI:** Iron Mountain's existing enterprise relationships across 225,000+ customers provide a built-in pipeline for cloud and AI infrastructure demand. Its 2025 acquisition of additional European data center assets positions it to capture the growing EU AI market.

**Risk to watch:** The legacy records storage business is a slow-declining revenue stream that weighs on the overall growth narrative.

## The Risks of Data Center REIT Investing

### Power Availability Is the Binding Constraint

The single biggest risk to data center growth is not demand — it is power. New data center campuses in Northern Virginia, the world's largest market, face 3-5 year wait times for utility power interconnection. Dominion Energy has warned that data center power demand in Virginia alone could require $30+ billion in grid upgrades over the next decade.

REITs that have pre-secured power allocations hold a meaningful competitive advantage. Those still seeking power face project delays and potentially lower returns on invested capital.

### Interest Rate Sensitivity

REITs are structurally leveraged businesses, and data center REITs are no exception. When interest rates rise, REIT borrowing costs increase, and the dividend yield spread over risk-free rates compresses, making REITs less attractive relative to bonds. The Fed's rate path remains data-dependent in 2026, and any hawkish surprise could pressure data center REIT valuations.

### Hyperscaler Self-Build Risk

Amazon, Google, Microsoft, and Meta are all building their own data centers alongside leasing from REITs. If hyperscalers shift more aggressively toward self-build, the growth in third-party leasing demand could decelerate. However, the current power and permitting bottlenecks make self-build timelines even longer than REIT development timelines in many markets, which limits this risk in the near term.

### Valuation Premium

Data center REITs trade at significant premiums to the broader REIT index. Equinix at 30x forward FFO compares to 15-17x for the REIT sector overall. This premium is justified by superior growth, but it also means any earnings disappointment or macro shock will hit data center REITs harder than the average property sector.

## How to Build a Data Center REIT Position in Your Portfolio

### Step 1: Determine Your Allocation

A 5-10% portfolio allocation to data center REITs provides meaningful exposure to AI-driven real estate growth without overconcentrating in a single sector. Investors with strong conviction and longer time horizons can go up to 15%, but should be comfortable with the higher volatility that comes with premium-valued growth REITs.

### Step 2: Choose Your Vehicle

- **Individual REITs** if you want to overweight specific operators (e.g., Equinix for interconnection, Digital Realty for hyperscale).
- **Data center ETFs** if you want diversified exposure and lower single-stock risk.
- **Broad REIT ETFs** if you want some data center exposure blended with other property sectors.

### Step 3: Account Placement Matters

REIT dividends are generally taxed as ordinary income, not at the qualified dividend rate. This makes REITs ideal candidates for tax-advantaged accounts (IRAs, 401(k)s, HSAs) where dividend taxation is deferred or eliminated.

If you hold REITs in a taxable account, you can offset some of the tax drag through the Section 199A deduction, which allows a 20% deduction on qualified REIT dividends for eligible taxpayers.

### Step 4: Dollar-Cost Average Into Position

Data center REITs can be volatile — 20-30% drawdowns have occurred even during bull markets when interest rate expectations shift. Dollar-cost averaging over 3-6 months reduces the risk of buying at a temporary peak.

## The Bottom Line

Data center REITs sit at the intersection of two of the most powerful secular trends in global markets: the AI compute explosion and the continued digitization of the global economy. Unlike speculative AI plays that depend on future revenue, data center REITs are generating real cash flows today from long-term leases with the most creditworthy tenants on earth.

The supply constraints — power, permitting, and construction timelines — create a multi-year window where demand will continue to outstrip supply, supporting above-average rent growth and occupancy rates. For investors looking for a tangible way to own the physical infrastructure of AI, data center REITs deserve serious consideration.

The key is sizing the position appropriately, placing it in the right account for tax efficiency, and understanding that premium valuations mean premium volatility. Get those right, and data center REITs offer a compelling blend of income and growth that few other asset classes can match in 2026.
